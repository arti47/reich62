# REICH '62
### A Genesys Narrative Dice System Manual
*Standalone — no other rulebook required*

**Setting:** Greater Germanic Reich, 1962. Operation Sealion succeeded (1941); Barbarossa delayed one year, launched with full Luftwaffe support; Wehrmacht reaches the Urals by 1943; Soviet remnant survives in Siberia. Britain: monarchy in exile, puppet regime in London, active resistance. United States: never drawn into the European war, isolationist, watching from across the Atlantic. By 1962 the Reich spans continental Europe to the Urals, with client states in France/Low Countries/Scandinavia and a fracturing internal power structure (Wehrmacht/SS/Party factionalism post-succession-crisis). Realistic 1960s tech only — no occult/super-science.

**Tone:** grim-oppressive resistance drama, pulpy adventure, and le Carré-style espionage tension, mixed per scene as the table wants.

**Party framework:** loose — any PC can be resistance, Reich operative, civilian, defector, or informant, mixed freely; supports solo or group play.

# PART I — CORE RULES

## 1. Dice

| Die | Type | Symbols generated |
|---|---|---|
| 🎲 Ability (green, d8) | Positive — from skill/characteristic | Success 🌟, Advantage 🔺 |
| 🎲 Proficiency (yellow, d12) | Positive — upgraded Ability | Success 🌟, Advantage 🔺, Triumph ☀️ |
| 💠 Difficulty (purple, d8) | Negative — task itself | Failure 💥, Threat 🔻 |
| 💠 Challenge (red, d12) | Negative — upgraded Difficulty | Failure 💥, Threat 🔻, Despair ⚡ |
| ▲ Boost (blue, d6) | Positive — situational | Success 🌟, Advantage 🔺, or blank |
| ▲ Setback (black, d6) | Negative — situational | Failure 💥, Threat 🔻, or blank |

**Resolving a roll:**
1. Cancel 🌟 against 💥 one-for-one.
2. Cancel 🔺 against 🔻 one-for-one.
3. Net 🌟 remaining = **success**. Net 💥 (or zero net 🌟) = **failure**.
4. Leftover 🔺/🔻 after cancellation = side-benefits or complications, narrated regardless of success/failure.
5. ☀️ Triumph and ⚡ Despair are **not** canceled by anything and are always true regardless of the check's overall success/failure — rare, dramatic swings.

## 2. Building a Dice Pool

1. **Skill + Characteristic:** compare the character's skill rank to the rank of the linked characteristic. The **higher** of the two values sets the number of Ability dice. Then **upgrade** a number of those dice into Proficiency dice equal to the **lower** value. (Unskilled = 0 upgrades.)
2. **Difficulty:** GM sets a difficulty level (Table 3 below) and adds that many Difficulty dice. GM may upgrade Difficulty→Challenge dice for skilled opposition or by spending a Story Point.
3. **Boost/Setback:** GM (or player, by argument) adds one Boost die per genuine situational advantage, one Setback die per genuine impediment. These do **not** cancel each other pre-roll — add all that apply.
4. **Modification order:** assemble base pool → add dice → upgrade dice → downgrade dice → remove dice. Always in that sequence.
5. Roll everything together and resolve per §1.

## 3. Difficulty

| Level | Difficulty dice | Guidance |
|---|---|---|
| Simple | none (auto-succeed or no roll) | Trivial |
| Easy | 💠 | Minor obstacle |
| Average | 💠💠 | Standard challenge |
| Hard | 💠💠💠 | Difficult task |
| Daunting | 💠💠💠💠 | Extreme task |
| Formidable | 💠💠💠💠💠 | Nearly impossible |
| Impossible | Formidable dice + costs 1 Story Point just to attempt | Reserve for critical/life-or-death story moments |

**Per-skill difficulty guidance (representative examples):**
| Skill | Easy | Average | Hard | Daunting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Athletics | Climb a short ladder | Scale a rough wall | Climb a sheer cliff | Free-climb an overhang under fire |
| Skulduggery | Pick a simple padlock | Pick a household lock | Pick a reinforced/complex lock | Pick a lock with no visible mechanism |
| Deception | Tell a simple, plausible lie | Lie under mild scrutiny | Lie to a trained interrogator | Lie to someone who already suspects the truth |
| Stealth | Sneak past a distracted guard | Sneak past an alert guard | Sneak past a vigilant patrol | Sneak past trained trackers/dogs |
| Streetwise | Find a common black-market good | Find a moderately illegal item | Find a restricted/military item | Find something the regime actively hunts for |
| Medicine | Treat a minor cut | Treat a serious wound | Perform field surgery | Perform surgery with improvised tools |
| Negotiation | Haggle over a minor price | Negotiate a standard deal | Negotiate with a hostile party | Negotiate life-or-death terms |

## 3A. Opposed, Competitive, and Assisted Checks

- **Opposed checks:** used when success/failure is directly contested by another character (social influence, sneaking past a guard, etc.). Build your pool normally, then build the "difficulty side" using the opposing character's relevant skill/characteristic exactly as you would build an ability pool — higher value sets Difficulty dice count, lower value upgrades that many to Challenge dice. Written as "opposed Skill vs. Skill" (e.g., opposed Deception vs. Vigilance).
- **Competitive checks:** used when multiple characters attempt the same task simultaneously and you need to rank outcomes (a footrace, competing negotiators). GM sets one Difficulty; everyone rolls against it; compare total uncanceled 🌟 — highest total wins/performs best. **Ties** break by total 🔺 (Advantage), then by Triumph (☀️); if still tied, treat as simultaneous. *(Errata R-3)*
- **Assisted checks:** see the Assist maneuver (§5A) — one character spends a maneuver to grant an engaged ally 🎲Boost on their next check; multiple assistants stack bonus dice, but unused dice are lost if not used immediately.

## 4. Characteristics & Skills

**Characteristics:** Brawn, Agility, Intellect, Cunning, Willpower, Presence.

**General skills:** Athletics(Br), Computers(Int), Cool(Pr), Coordination(Ag), Discipline(Will), Driving(Ag), Mechanics(Int), Medicine(Int), Perception(Cun), Piloting(Ag), Resilience(Br), Skulduggery(Cun), Stealth(Ag), Streetwise(Cun), Survival(Cun), Vigilance(Will).

**Social skills:** Charm(Pr), Coercion(Will), Deception(Cun), Leadership(Pr), Negotiation(Pr).

**Knowledge skills:** Knowledge(Int) — specialize by GM/player agreement (e.g., Reich Bureaucracy, Geography, Underworld).

**Combat skills:** Brawl(Br), Melee(Br), Ranged(Ag), Gunnery(Ag) [for mounted/vehicle weapons].

**Not used in Reich '62:** Alchemy, Astrocartography, Operating, Riding, Arcana, Divine, Primal (no magic, no exotic settings).

## 5. Combat Sequence (structured encounters)

1. **Initiative:** each participant makes a Simple(–) Cool or Vigilance check (Cool if the encounter began through planning/anticipation; Vigilance if it began by surprise/ambush — default to Vigilance if unsure). Rank all results by uncanceled 🌟 (ties broken by 🔺, then PC-before-NPC). This produces an ordered list of **Initiative slots**, each tagged as either a "PC slot" (won by a player character's roll) or an "NPC slot" (won by an NPC's roll) — see §5A' below for how slots are filled.

### 5A'. Initiative Slot Filling
Initiative order and slot ownership are set once at the start of the encounter and don't change round to round. Each round, working down the ordered slot list: if the slot is a **PC slot**, the players collectively choose which not-yet-acted PC takes it; if it's an **NPC slot**, the GM chooses which not-yet-acted NPC takes it. This lets players sequence their own turns adaptively within their side's slots, while the GM does the same for NPCs — it is not a fixed per-character order.

2. **Turn budget:** 1 action + up to 2 maneuvers per turn. **§5A governs the exact maneuver economy** *(Errata R-21)*: the first maneuver is free, a second costs 2 strain, and 2 is the hard cap regardless of strain spent.
3. **Maneuver:** free-ish setup — move a range band, aim, take cover. Usually no roll.
4. **Action:** the significant act — attack, skilled check, etc. Only one per turn (barring talents).
5. **Incidental:** near-free minor acts (drop item, call out) — effectively unlimited within reason.
6. **Combat check:** assemble pool (attacker's combat skill+characteristic vs Difficulty set by target's defense/GM). On success, damage = weapon base damage + 1 per uncanceled 🌟, minus target's Soak = wounds (or strain, for certain effects).
7. **Critical Injury trigger:** when uncanceled 🔺 meets/exceeds the weapon's Critical rating, or by spending 🔺🔺 or an uncanceled ⚡, roll d100 on the Critical Injury Table (§9).
8. Round ends when all participants have acted; repeat until the encounter resolves.

## 5A. Maneuvers (full list)

One free maneuver per turn; a second costs 2 strain (or is granted by spending 🔺🔺 on a check, or other specific abilities). Never more than two maneuvers per turn (bonus maneuvers granted out-of-turn by GM discretion don't count toward this cap).

- **Aim:** steady a weapon before attacking. One maneuver → 🎲Boost on next combat check (two consecutive → 🎲Boost🎲Boost). Alternatively, aim to target a specific item/limb: one maneuver → 🎲Setback🎲Setback on next combat check; two consecutive → 🎲Setback only. Lost if the character moves, acts otherwise, or takes damage exceeding soak before the next check.
- **Assist:** an engaged ally performing this maneuver adds 🎲Boost to your next check; multiple assisting allies stack bonus dice. Unused bonus dice are lost if not used on your very next turn.
- **Guarded Stance:** add 🎲Setback to all your combat checks until the end of your next turn, but gain melee defense 1 for the same duration.
- **Interact with the Environment:** open/close a door, flip a table, take cover (ranged defense 1, or more for very sturdy cover), and similar single-maneuver environmental actions.
- **Manage Gear:** draw/holster/ready/load a weapon; retrieve or stow an item from storage.
- **Mount or Dismount:** enter/exit a vehicle or mount a domesticated animal (mounting an untrained animal instead requires an Average Survival check as an action).
- **Move:** change range increment (short↔medium = 1 maneuver; medium↔long or long↔extreme = 2 maneuvers); engage/disengage an opponent; reposition within short range.
- **Drop Prone / Stand from Prone:** dropping prone grants 🎲Boost vs. ranged attacks but 🎲Setback vs. melee attacks against you.
- **Preparation:** setup maneuver required by certain talents/abilities before their use ("prepare" in their text).

## 5B. Actions (full list)

One action per turn (barring abilities that grant more). Four types:
1. **Exchange action for a maneuver** — gain a 2nd maneuver instead of your action (still capped at 2 maneuvers total).
2. **Spend action to activate an ability/talent** — as specified by the talent.
3. **Perform a skill check** — any task with meaningful chance/stakes of failure.
4. **Perform a combat check** — a skill check with the additional steps below.

**Combat check procedure:**
1. Declare attack, select skill/weapon, declare target.
2. Assemble dice pool (skill+characteristic vs. Difficulty). Melee attacks are always Average(💠💠); ranged attack Difficulty depends on range (Table 5B-1).
3. Roll; on success, damage = weapon base + 1 per uncanceled 🌟.
4. Resolve 🔺/☀️ (attacker's choice — see §5C).
5. Resolve 🔻/⚡ (GM's choice by default — see §5C).
6. Reduce damage by target's Soak; remainder = wounds. Trigger Critical Injury if the weapon's Crit rating is met by uncanceled 🔺, by spending 🔺🔺, or by an uncanceled ⚡ — roll d100 on §9.

**Table 5B-1: Ranged Attack Difficulty by Range**
| Range | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Engaged | Easy(💠) + weapon-specific modifiers (see §12.2 crossover w/ melee-while-engaged) |
| Short | Easy(💠) |
| Medium | Average(💠💠) |
| Long | Hard(💠💠💠) |
| Extreme | Daunting(💠💠💠💠) |

## 5C. Combat Advantage/Threat Spend Tables

**Reading these tables** *(Errata R-12):* a row like "🔺🔺 or ☀️" means a single Triumph/Despair can buy that row's effect outright — it does **not** mean Triumph is worth exactly two Advantage. Advantage/Threat costs remain literal (must accumulate the stated count); Triumph/Despair can buy **any** row in the table regardless of tier, at the spender's choice.


**Spending 🔺/☀️ (attacker's choice, in combat):**
| Cost | Effect |
|---|---|
| 🔺 or ☀️ | Recover 1 strain; add 🎲Boost to an ally's next check; notice a useful tactical detail; inflict a Critical Injury on a successful damaging hit (cost varies by weapon); activate an item quality (cost varies) |
| 🔺🔺 or ☀️ | Perform an immediate free maneuver (still capped at 2/turn); add 🎲Boost to the target's or an ally's next check |
| 🔺🔺🔺 or ☀️ | Negate the target's defense (cover/equipment/Guarded Stance) until end of round; ignore penalizing environmental effects until your next turn; disable the target/gear instead of dealing damage (GM-agreed); gain +1 melee/ranged defense until your next turn; force target to drop a weapon |
| ☀️ | Upgrade difficulty of target's next check; upgrade an ally's next check's ability; do something vital (e.g. seal a door); on Initiative, gain an immediate free maneuver before combat begins |
| ☀️☀️ | Destroy a piece of the target's equipment |

**Spending 🔻/⚡ (GM's choice by default):**
| Cost | Effect |
|---|---|
| 🔻 or ⚡ | Active character suffers 1 strain; loses the benefit of a prior maneuver (cover, Guarded Stance) until repeated |
| 🔻🔻 or ⚡ | Opponent gets an immediate free maneuver as an incidental; target gains 🎲Boost; active/ally suffers 🎲Setback on next action |
| 🔻🔻🔻 or ⚡ | Active character falls prone; active character grants the enemy a significant tactical advantage |
| ⚡ | Weapon runs out of ammunition for the rest of the encounter; upgrade difficulty of an ally's/active character's next check; active character's weapon/tool becomes damaged |

## 5C'. Generic Advantage/Threat Spend Guide (non-combat, non-social checks)

For skill checks outside combat (§5C) and social encounters (§11) — Mechanics, Perception, Stealth, Streetwise, Survival, and similar — there's no fixed table; interpret 🔺/☀️/🔻/⚡ narratively using this scaling guide:

| Cost | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| 🔺 or ☀️ | A minor positive side-detail: finish faster, notice something extra, leave no trace, gain 🎲Boost on a related follow-up check |
| 🔺🔺 or ☀️ | A meaningful bonus: an ally benefits too, a complication is avoided entirely, gather bonus information |
| 🔺🔺🔺 or ☀️ | A significant advantageous swing: something unexpected and valuable happens as a direct result |
| 🔻 or ⚡ | A minor complication: takes longer, draws minor attention, minor cost incurred |
| 🔻🔻 or ⚡ | A meaningful complication: equipment strain/wear, an ally is inconvenienced, a witness notices |
| 🔻🔻🔻 or ⚡ | A significant complication: a real setback — in Reich '62, this is a common trigger point for Heat (§17.1) when the check occurred in a surveilled context |

## 5C''. Multiple Attackers / Defenders

Genesys has no fixed numeric "flanking" bonus; represent being outnumbered or ganging up via situational dice, applied by the GM:
- **Defender facing multiple attackers:** typically no automatic penalty for the first extra attacker, but the GM may add 🎲Setback to the defender's own next check if they're being meaningfully overwhelmed (3+ engaged attackers), representing divided attention.
- **Attackers ganging up on one target:** the GM may grant an attacking group 🎲Boost when their target is already engaged with an ally (distracted, flanked), especially in melee.
- **Reich '62 default:** don't stack these automatically — reserve them for scenes where being surrounded/outnumbered is genuinely dramatic, to avoid bogging down fast narrative combat.

## 5D. Range Bands (detail)

Five bands: **Engaged** (subcategory of short — close enough to directly interact/melee), **Short** (a few meters; comfortable conversation distance; most movement within it is one maneuver), **Medium** (tens of meters; raised-voice conversation; one maneuver from short), **Long** (tens-to-hundreds of meters; requires yelling; two maneuvers from medium), **Extreme** (farthest interactive range; may be inaudible even shouting; two maneuvers from long). Moving the full engaged→extreme span typically requires more maneuvers than a single turn allows.

## 5E. Environmental Effects

- **Concealment** (darkness, smoke, fog, tall grass): adds 🎲Boost to Stealth for the concealed character, and equal 🎲Setback to ranged attacks/Perception/Vigilance targeting them. +1 die (mist, shadow) / +2 (fog, dusk/dawn, thick grass) / +3 (heavy fog, smoke, night, dense underbrush).
- **Cover:** grants ranged defense 1 (more for prepared positions like trenches/bunkers) plus 🎲Boost to relevant checks (e.g. Perception while behind it).
- **Difficult terrain:** doubles the maneuvers needed to cover the same distance. **Impassable terrain:** cannot be crossed by maneuver alone — requires an Athletics/Coordination action (or narrative workaround).
- **Water:** treated as difficult terrain by default; swift/stormy water may require an Athletics action to cross. Submerged characters hold breath for rounds equal to Brawn before suffocating.
- **Fire/acid/corrosive atmosphere:** GM assigns a rating (1–10+); character suffers wounds equal to the rating each round exposed, at the start of their turn, until removed from the hazard.
- **Extreme cold/heat, thin air, etc.:** GM-adjudicated, generally via added 🎲Setback dice to relevant physical checks per severity — treat identically to concealment's dice-added model.

## 5F. Encumbrance

- Every item has an encumbrance value (0–6 typical; small items 1-2, medium 3-4, large 5-6). Ten loosely-carried incidentals = 1 Encum; twenty well-organized incidentals (pockets, pouches) = 1 Encum.
- **Encumbrance Threshold** = 5 + Brawn. Exceeding it: 🎲Setback on all Agility/Brawn checks per point over.
- If encumbered by an amount ≥ Brawn rating, lose the free maneuver each turn (each of up to 2 maneuvers now costs 2 strain).
- Lifting something over threshold: Athletics check, Easy(💠) for 1 over, +1 difficulty per additional point over, capped at Daunting(💠💠💠💠) for 4+ over. Allies helping lift add their Brawn to the effective threshold for that task.

## 5G. Recovery & Healing

- **Natural rest:** heal 1 wound per full night's rest, regardless of current state. Per full week of rest, attempt a Resilience check (difficulty = the Critical Injury's severity) to also heal one Critical Injury; on ☀️ an additional Critical Injury heals too. *(Errata R-9 — corrected from ⚡, which would contradict §1's rule that Despair is never beneficial)*
- **Medical care (Medicine check):** once per encounter per target. Difficulty by state of health — Easy(💠) if current wounds ≤ half threshold, Average(💠💠) if > half, Hard(💠💠💠) if wounds exceed threshold; Critical Injury severity rating for treating a Critical Injury (once/week/injury). Heals wounds = uncanceled 🌟, strain = uncanceled 🔺. Self-treatment: +2 difficulty. No medical equipment: +1 difficulty.
- **Painkillers (consumables):** 1st use heals 5 wounds, 2nd heals 4, 3rd heals 3... 6th+ heals 0. Never heals Critical Injuries. Costs one maneuver to administer (self or ally, if engaged).
- **Strain recovery:** at end of any encounter, Simple(–) Discipline or Cool check; heal 1 strain per uncanceled 🌟. A full night's rest clears all strain.
- Critical Injuries persist (mechanically) even after their listed effect duration ends, until properly treated — each additional Critical Injury adds +10 to future Critical Injury rolls, stacking danger.

## 5H. Two-Weapon & Unarmed Combat

- **Two-weapon combat:** designate a primary (one-handed) weapon; compare skill/characteristic ranks used for each weapon and use the lower of each; compare each weapon's normal Difficulty and use the higher, then increase by one more. On success, hit with the primary automatically; spend 🔺🔺 or ☀️ to also hit with the secondary. Each hit deals its own weapon's damage.
- **Unarmed combat (Brawl):** base damage = Brawn, range Engaged, Crit 5, Knockdown quality; may target strain instead of wounds. Brawl weapons (knuckledusters etc.) add to this base rather than replacing it.
- **Improvised weapons:** use Melee skill, add Brawn to damage, generate one automatic 🔻 on any check (Inferior-quality equivalent); break on a 🔻🔻 or 🔺🔺... check individual weapon table for size-based stats.

## 5I. Falling & Suffocation

**Falling damage:** GM matches fall distance to the closest range band, then consults:
| Fall distance | Wounds | Strain |
|---|---|---|
| Short | 10 | 10 |
| Medium | 30 | 20 |
| Long | Wounds = Wound Threshold+1; Critical Injury roll +50 | 30 |
| Extreme | Wounds = Wound Threshold+1; Critical Injury roll +75 (or death, GM discretion) | 40 |

Wound damage is reduced by soak; strain is not. An Average Athletics/Coordination check can reduce the fall: 1 wound reduced per uncanceled 🌟, 1 strain reduced per uncanceled 🔺, and an uncanceled ☀️ may reduce the effective fall by one range band (grabbing a handhold, etc.).

**Suffocation:** 3 strain per round at the start of the suffocating character's turn until resolved. Once strain exceeds Strain Threshold, incapacitated; each subsequent turn while still suffocating inflicts one additional Critical Injury roll, until the character stops suffocating or dies.

## 6. Derived Attributes

**Human base thresholds** 🏷️ *(Errata R-1 — an inferred value: neither source printed a base explicitly; 8/10 is the pair the §16 pregens' printed stats agree on):*
| | Base | + Characteristic | = Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wound Threshold | **8** | + Brawn | e.g. Brawn 2 → 10 |
| Strain Threshold | **10** | + Willpower | e.g. Willpower 2 → 12 |

- **Wound Threshold** = species base (8, human) + Brawn. Fixed at character creation; only raised afterward via talents such as Toughened.
- **Strain Threshold** = species base (10, human) + Willpower. Fixed at creation; raised via talents such as Grit.
- **Soak** = Brawn + armor bonus (rises automatically with Brawn increases, unlike the two thresholds above).
- **Defense** (melee/ranged) = 0 by default; gained from armor, cover, or talents.
- **Incapacitated** when Wounds ≥ Wound Threshold or Strain ≥ Strain Threshold.

## 7. Experience Points (XP)

- **Characteristic increase** (creation only): all six characteristics start at rating **1**; raise via 10× the new rating, purchased sequentially. Max rating 5 for PCs at creation. *(Errata R-5)*
- **Career skill rank:** 5× the new rank.
- **Non-career skill rank:** (5× the new rank) + 5.
- **Talent:** 5× its tier (Tier 1 = 5XP … Tier 5 = 25XP). A character must hold at least as many talents in tier N−1 as in tier N before buying into tier N.
- Skill ranks capped at 2 during character creation regardless of source (unless a rule states otherwise); talents/later play can raise further.

## 8. Story Points (economy)

A shared pool that flows between GM and players as scenes resolve favorably or unfavorably.

- **Starting pool:** typically 1 Story Point per player character (some tables use 2), all placed in the **Light/Player pool**. **The Dark/GM pool starts at 0** — it only fills as players spend from their pool and the point flows across per the rule below. *(Errata R-4)*
- **Spending from the Player pool:** upgrade or downgrade one die once on a check, add a Boost or Setback die, narrate a minor established detail into the fiction, or (spending 1) attempt an otherwise-Impossible check.
- **Spending from the GM pool:** same options, applied against the players (upgrade a Difficulty die, add a Setback die, narrate a complication, etc.).
- **Flow:** when a player spends a Story Point, it moves to the GM's pool once its effect resolves. When the GM spends one, it moves to the players' pool. Certain Critical Injury results (Discouraging Wound, §9) also shift a point between pools directly.
- **Session reset:** unspent Story Points typically carry over between sessions rather than resetting, unless the GM rules otherwise for pacing reasons.
- **Push (reroll):** a player may spend 1 Story Point to reroll an **entire pool** after a check (failed, or a success they want to improve). Every uncanceled 🔻 or ⚡ on the reroll beyond what the original roll generated inflicts, player's choice: 1 Heat (if surveilled context, §17.1), 1 point of gear damage (§14B Minor tier), or 1 strain. Triumph/Despair on the reroll are read fresh. A check can only be pushed once.

## 8A. Clocks (generalized countdown/progress track)

A **Clock** is a named track with a size — 4, 6, or 8 segments — and a direction (filling toward a triggered consequence, or the reverse for a "safety" clock counting down). Ticked by symbols a resolved check already produced:

| Symbol | Effect on the clock |
|---|---|
| 🔻 (Threat) | Fills 1 segment |
| ⚡ (Despair) | Fills 2 segments |
| 🌟 (Success) | Fills 1 segment (only for progress-type clocks where success advances the goal) |
| Every 2× 🔺 (Advantage) | Fills 1 additional segment (optional, GM discretion) |
| ☀️ (Triumph) | Fills the acting character's own clock by its full remaining need, **or** clears 1 segment from an opposing/threat clock (player's choice) |

When a clock fills completely, its stated consequence triggers immediately — no extra roll needed.

**Reich '62's named tracks are all instances of this pattern**, kept under their own names for clarity rather than folded together:
- **Heat (§17)** = a size-5 clock, filled per §17.1's specific rules.
- **Personal Threat Countdown (§33)** = a size-3 clock, but advanced by **GM discretion** (not the symbol-fill table above) — see §33 for its own pacing rule.
- **Dragnet (Bestiary, Manhunt/Dragnet)** = a size-4 scaling clock.
- **Stop Countdown (§34)** = a d10 event table rather than a fill-based clock, since it generates *content* rather than tracking progress toward one fixed consequence — kept separate for that reason.

**Use this generalized version whenever you invent new countdown-style content on the fly** (a bomb timer, a rescue window, an interrogation resistance track) — pick a size, decide what filling it means, and let the symbols already on the table do the work, rather than designing a bespoke mechanic each time.

## 11. Social Encounters

Social encounters use **narrative**, not structured, gameplay — no strict turn order unless the GM imposes rounds (recommended for complex, multi-NPC negotiations to ensure everyone gets a turn).

- **Goal-based:** GM defines the party's goal at the start; the encounter ends on clear success or clear failure.
- **Three resolution paths:**
  1. **Mutually agreeable solution** — no roll needed if the offered terms are simply acceptable (GM discretion; may still grant 🎲Boost elsewhere for good roleplay).
  2. **Opposed social skill check** — the PC's social skill vs. the target's opposing skill (usually Discipline or Cool). This is the default/recommended method.
  3. **Group influence** — set Difficulty by audience size: 2–5 targets Average(💠💠), 6–15 Hard(💠💠💠), 16–50 Daunting(💠💠💠💠), 51+ Formidable(💠💠💠💠💠).

For solo play or fast GM improvisation, the **Conversation Generator (§40)** provides a quick subject and Tilt-based modifier for scenes without pre-scripted dialogue.

## 12. Vehicle & Chase Rules

*Period-adapted for Reich '62: cars, motorcycles, trucks, trains. No starship/capital-ship-scale content used.*

**Vehicle characteristics:**
- **Handling:** ± modifier adding 🎲Boost (positive) or 🎲Setback (negative) to Driving/Piloting checks.
- **Speed:** current value, 0 up to the vehicle's maximum; changed via Accelerate/Decelerate maneuvers.
- **Silhouette:** size category — motorcycle 1, car 2, truck 2–3, train 4+.
- **Defense/Armor:** the vehicle's soak-equivalent.
- **Hull Trauma Threshold:** the vehicle's "wound threshold."
- **System Strain Threshold:** the vehicle's "strain threshold" (overheating, mechanical failure). Recovers 1/day if undamaged, or via the Damage Control action.

**Combat/chase sequence:** uses the standard structured combat rules (§5) plus:
- **Vehicle maneuvers:** Accelerate, Decelerate, Evade, Reposition, Brace for Impact.
- **Vehicle actions:** Perform a Combat Check (ram/shoot), Dangerous Driving, Gain the Advantage, Damage Control.
- The pilot uses Driving/Piloting; passengers act independently (e.g., shoot from a window) on their own normal turns.
- **Scale note:** skip planetary-scale/strategic-range conversions entirely — treat all vehicle weapon damage as personal scale, using the standard 5 range bands.

**Crashes:** losing control (failed Driving/Piloting check with ⚡, or GM call) → vehicle suffers hull trauma equal to current speed; occupants may suffer wounds/a Critical Injury roll per GM ruling (treat as fall/impact damage). For a more granular outcome, roll on the **Vehicle Component Damage Table (§37)** instead of flat hull trauma.

**Mixed personal/vehicle combat:** on-foot characters engaging vehicles (or vice versa) act on their own normal turns while vehicles act on the pilot's; smaller targets on foot benefit from the standard silhouette rule (§5J) — attacking something 2+ silhouettes larger than you is easier (−1 difficulty), so infantry can hit vehicles more easily than they can meaningfully damage them.

**Vehicle Combat Advantage/Threat Spends** (adapts §5C for vehicle actions):
| Cost | Effect |
|---|---|
| 🔺 or ☀️ | Reduce system strain by 1; a passenger notices something useful; minor positioning advantage |
| 🔺🔺 or ☀️ | Free Pilot-Only maneuver without the normal system-strain cost; force the target vehicle off its intended course |
| 🔺🔺🔺 or ☀️ | Disable a component instead of dealing hull trauma (tire, engine, weapon mount); gain a full extra range-band of separation |
| 🔻 or ⚡ | Vehicle suffers 1 system strain; lose the benefit of a prior vehicle maneuver |
| 🔻🔻 or ⚡ | Vehicle control check required next turn or lose a range increment of positioning; a passenger is jostled (1 strain) |
| 🔻🔻🔻 or ⚡ | Vehicle stalls/skids — treat as losing control (§12 Crashes) |
| ⚡ | A component breaks (per §14B Item Maintenance, treat as Major damage) |

## 12B. Motivation Tables (Desire / Fear / Strength / Flaw)

Each PC selects or rolls (d100, or d10×10 read as tens digit) one of each. Discuss specifics with the group; reflavor for occupation-era stakes as needed.

**Fear (d100):** Change (loss of stability) · Commitment (being relied upon) · Death · Expression (a hidden private truth) · Failure · Humiliation (being seen as wrong/foolish) · Isolation (dying alone) · Nemesis (a specific dreaded foe) · Obscurity (being forgotten) · Poverty (being without).

## 12C. Adversary Building (Minion / Rival / Nemesis)

Three NPC profile types, each a simplified PC framework:

**Minions** — faceless, disposable, deployed in groups.
- No strain track (anything that would inflict strain inflicts wounds instead); cannot voluntarily suffer strain.
- No individual skills — a group gains 1 rank in each profile-listed skill per member beyond the first.
- A group shares one Wound Threshold = sum of individual thresholds; attacks target the group, not individuals; each time cumulative wounds exceed one member's share, one minion drops.
- Any Critical Injury result instantly incapacitates one minion (group suffers that minion's wound share + 1).
- Threat guide: 1 minion ≈ negligible; 2–3 ≈ reasonable threat to 1 starting PC; 3–4 ≈ reasonable for a PC with 100+ XP spent.

**Rivals** — named, skilled roughly at starting-PC level (rarely 2+ ranks in any skill), operate alone.
- Suffer Critical Injuries normally; GM may rule a rival is killed outright (not just incapacitated) when their Wound Threshold is exceeded.
- No strain track — strain-inflicting effects instead inflict equivalent wounds.
- Threat guide: 1 rival ≈ reasonably dangerous to 1 PC; scale count to party size. Soak 5+, Wound Threshold 14+, 3+ combat/social ranks, or 9+ damage weapons push a rival to "very challenging."

**Nemeses** — full PC-equivalent antagonists; may exceed PC power levels. Suffer strain and wounds normally, with no special simplification — build them essentially as you would a PC, often with unique named talents/abilities.

**Profile fields:** Name/type, Description, six Characteristics, Soak/Defense/Thresholds (nemeses only get a Strain Threshold), Skills, Talents, Abilities (unique, non-skill/talent effects), Equipment (only notable gear — mundane pocket litter is assumed, not listed).

All adversaries have access to the **Adversary talent** (passive, ranked): upgrade the difficulty of all combat checks targeting them once per rank — the standard "hard to hit" NPC talent, replacing PC-only defensive talents.

## 12D. Adversary Special Abilities (Reich '62 catalog)

NPC-only abilities layered on top of the Minion/Rival/Nemesis framework, used sparingly for named or distinctive antagonists:

- **Fear X (passive):** characters engaging this adversary in melee for the first time each encounter must succeed at a Discipline check (difficulty = X) or suffer 🎲Setback on their combat checks until the end of their next turn. Use for a genuinely dreaded SD interrogator or similarly notorious figure.
- **Interrogator's Eye (passive):** adds automatic 🔺 to this NPC's Perception/Discipline checks made specifically to detect lies or forged documents.
- **Chain of Command (active, incidental, once/encounter):** this NPC may direct one nearby Minion group to immediately perform a free maneuver as an out-of-turn incidental.
- **Hardened (passive):** immune to the Disorient and Stagger effects of Critical Injuries — reflects trained composure under pressure (elite security personnel).
- **Web of Informants (passive):** reduce this NPC's effective Rarity when the party attempts to identify or locate them by 2 (they're well-connected and hard to isolate).
- **Environmental Affinity — Urban (passive):** removes 🎲Setback from this NPC's Stealth and Streetwise checks made within city environments (native operators, black-market veterans).
- **Ruthless (passive):** once per encounter, this NPC may reroll a failed Coercion or combat check — representing willingness to escalate past normal limits.

Use these to differentiate named Rivals/Nemeses from generic stat blocks without inflating their full skill/characteristic profile.

Local officials, police, or informants who work with the regime — for survival, advantage, or belief. A collaborator PC is a live wire at the table: capable of betrayal, redemption, or both. Suggested Motivation: Fear — losing status/protection; Flaw — self-justification.

Each career: pick 4 of its 8 listed skills, gain rank 1 in each, before spending starting XP (standard Genesys career-skill rule).

## 14A. Rarity & Purchasing System

Rarity measures how hard an item is to find, 0 (trivial) to 10 (nearly impossible); the GM always has final narrative say over availability. All prices in this manual are in **Reichsmark (RM)**; starting budget is 500 RM (§13). *(Errata R-8)*

| Rarity | Difficulty to find | Reich '62 examples |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Simple(–) | Pencil, ration book |
| 2–3 | Easy(💠) | Pocketknife, bicycle |
| 4–5 | Average(💠💠) | Pistol, passenger sedan, basic forged papers |
| 6–7 | Hard(💠💠💠) | Restricted radio, shortwave set, good forged papers |
| 8–9 | Daunting(💠💠💠💠) | Military-grade weapon in civilian hands, excellent forged papers |
| 10 | Formidable(💠💠💠💠💠) | Armored vehicle, direct access to Reich classified files |

Make a Negotiation (legal goods) or Streetwise (black-market/illegal goods) check at the Rarity's difficulty to locate an item.

**Modifiers** (adjust effective Rarity before rolling): −1 major city/trading hub; +0 mid-size city; +1 rural/state-controlled economy; +2 frontier/restricted-ownership item; +3 active security crackdown; +4 disaster/total lockdown zone. Rarity above 10 stays Formidable, but the GM may upgrade the difficulty once per point beyond 10.

**Selling:** Negotiation (legal) or Streetwise (black-market) check at the item's Rarity difficulty; sell for 1/4 price on success, 1/2 with 🌟🌟, 3/4 with 🌟🌟🌟+.

**🏷️ House rule — Black-Market Purchase Mechanic (not derived from any printed Genesys rule):** for Rarity 6+ items, the GM may require Ration Cards or a barter good in addition to RM — spend 1 Ration Card unit per Rarity point above 5 (e.g., Rarity 7 = 2 cards). No cards on hand → +1 difficulty instead. A failed check with 🔻🔻🔻/⚡ is a surveilled-context check (§17.1) — black-market deals can generate Heat like any other public check. *(Errata H-1)*

An uncanceled 🔻🔻🔻 (or ⚡) on a check using a weapon/tool may indicate it malfunctions, jams, or breaks — GM's call, per §5C.

|---|---|---|---|

## 14C. Item Attachments & Hard Points (optional customization)

- **Hard points** = half the item's base encumbrance, rounded up (Encum 1–2 → 1 hard point; 3–4 → 2; 5–6 → 3, etc.). Calculate from base encumbrance, before any attachment modifiers.
- **Attachments** occupy a number of hard points when installed; once installed, those hard points are no longer free.
- **Installation:** roughly one hour of work plus a successful Average(💠💠) Mechanics check. Failure means the attachment isn't installed (retry allowed); failure with 🔻🔻 destroys the attachment; success with 🔻 means it works but may fail at an inopportune later moment (GM's call).
- **Reich '62 examples:** a suppressor (1 hard point, 🎲Boost to Stealth checks after firing but −1 damage), a scope (1 hard point, remove 🎲Setback at Long/Extreme range), a hidden compartment on a vehicle (2 hard points, conceal small items from a routine search).

# PART III — REICH '62 SUBSYSTEMS

## 17. Suspicion/Heat System

**Simplified to a single shared track (0–5)** — one Heat value for the whole party/cell, rather than tracking Personal and Cell Heat separately. This trades away individual-PC nuance for less bookkeeping; see §17.5 for when to bring the split back.

### 17.1 Generation
- Triggered only on checks made **in Reich-surveilled contexts** (public spaces, checkpoints, dealings with officials/informants).
- Uncanceled **Despair (⚡)** on such a check → **Heat +1**.
- Uncanceled Despair on an **evasion-type check** (Deception, Skulduggery, Streetwise, or Cool used specifically to evade/mislead surveillance) → **Heat +2**.
- Uncanceled **Triumph (☀️)** → any player may optionally spend it to reduce Heat by 1.
- Routine Threat/Advantage does **not** generate Heat — those remain reserved for immediate narrative complications only.

### 17.2 Thresholds

| Level | Effect |
|---|---|
| 1 | 🎲Setback die on public checks |
| 2 | Papers checked on sight (opposed Deception/Cool vs. Perception) |
| 3 | Tailed (opposed Vigilance to notice) / a shared safehouse placed under watch |
| 4 | An informant is assigned to the cell; a residence is searched; Oracle roll may determine a member is flipped or arrested |
| 5 | Raid imminent (Oracle determines timing) — cell burned, network collapse (GM narrates) |

**Safehouse status (tri-state, tied to the same track):** every safehouse the cell uses is in one of three states — **Clear** (Heat 0–2, no special effect), **Watched** (Heat 3–4, per the threshold effect above — assume any activity there may be observed), or **Blown** (Heat 5 — the location is compromised and must be abandoned). Track this per-safehouse if the cell uses more than one; a raid or relocation can reset a specific safehouse to Clear without resetting the whole Heat track.

### 17.3 Decay
- −1 Heat per session of low-risk downtime, or via specific actions (bribery, new papers, relocating/disappearing, "lying low").

### 17.4 Attributing a spike
Since the track is shared, when Heat rises the GM should still narrate *which PC's action* caused it — the mechanical consequence is shared, but the fictional cause and any immediate fallout (that PC specifically being recognized, followed, or named) should land on the responsible character first, even though the number on the sheet is common to the party.

### 17.5 When to bring back the Personal/Cell split
Restore the two-track version (Personal Heat 0–5 per character, Cell Heat 0–5 shared, Cell rising when any Personal Heat hits 3+) if: the party is 4+ PCs who plausibly act semi-independently, or "one member's carelessness endangering everyone else" is a dynamic you specifically want to play out. In that case, use the original threshold table structure — Personal effects (papers checks, tails, searches) apply to the individual; Cell effects (safehouse watched, network-wide consequences) apply to everyone.

## 18. Oracle System (for solo or GM-optional play)

### 18.1 Procedure
1. Frame the question and set a likelihood: **Likely / 50-50 / Unlikely**.
2. Roll:
   - Likely: 2 Ability 🎲 vs. 1 Difficulty 💠
   - 50-50: 2🎲 vs. 2💠
   - Unlikely: 1🎲 vs. 2💠
3. Interpret using §1's resolution rules:
   - Net success (🌟) → **Yes**
   - Net failure (💥) → **No**
   - **Two or more net 🌟 with no 🔻 left over** → **Yes, and...** (favorable complication — roll a Random Event, §19)
   - **Two or more net 💥 with no 🔺 left over** → **No, and...** (adverse complication — roll a Random Event, §19; feeds Heat if the question concerned a surveilled context)
   - Net 🔺/🔻 only, no net success/failure → **Yes, but... / No, but...** (interpret narratively)

**Errata note (R-22):** this pool is Ability vs. Difficulty dice — it cannot roll Triumph (Proficiency-only) or Despair (Challenge-only), per §1's face table. "Yes, and" / "No, and" are therefore read by **magnitude** (2+ net Success/Failure with nothing left over on the other side) rather than by Triumph/Despair symbols. If the pool is ever upgraded (e.g., by spending a Story Point per §8) and an actual Triumph or Despair does appear, it still reads the same way — as an emphatic "and" result.

## 18A. Fate Question Focus (optional house aid — H-2)

**🏷️ Not a printed rule** — layers on top of §18's Oracle without replacing it. The Oracle still answers yes or no; this reads that same roll's leftover Advantage/Threat as a gauge of how the answer compares to what you expected before asking.

**Procedure:** after resolving the Oracle roll (§18.1) normally, look at the **net 🔺/🔻 remaining** (−4 to +4 across the printed pools) and consult:

| Net | Band | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| −4 | That is a surprise | Lands well away from what you expected, and not in your favor. *(also triggers a Random Event, §19)* |
| −3 | Works against you | Read as expected, then bend the reading until it costs your character. |
| −2 | As expected, but... | What you expected, with something significant taking the edge off it. |
| −1 | Not quite what you expected | Close to expected, off in a detail — and the detail isn't helpful. |
| 0 | As you expected | Read the answer the way you expected. *(see Chaos Tiebreaker below)* |
| +1 | Not quite what you expected | Close to expected, off in a detail — and the detail suits you. |
| +2 | As expected, and... | What you expected, with something significant coming along with it. |
| +3 | In your favor | Read as expected, then bend the reading until it works out for your character. |
| +4 | A turn in the story | Read as expected, then bend the reading until it changes where an arc is heading — your way. *(also triggers a Random Event, §19)* |

**Chaos Tiebreaker (net = 0 only):** when Advantage and Threat cancel exactly, nothing on the roll itself says which way to lean — so the campaign's own escalation dial decides. Roll 1d10 against **2× current Heat** (§17): result at or under that value bends the answer to "Works against you"; above it, the answer stands as expected. At Heat 0, skip the roll entirely — the answer simply stands as expected.

## 19. Random Event Table

**Trigger:** either emphatic Oracle answer — "Yes, and" or "No, and" (§18.1) — or on-demand for solo scene-framing.

1. Roll 1d10 for **Category**: 1–2 NPC Action / 3–4 Reich Interference / 5–6 Complication or Twist / 7–8 Ally or Opportunity / 9–10 Remote Event (world-state shift).
2. Roll 1d10 for **Subject**: 1–3 current PC/scene / 4–6 Cell/Heat state / 7–8 a prior unresolved thread / 9–10 a new element (interpret freely).
3. **Skew:** an emphatic "Yes, and" skews the event favorable (read it as an opening); an emphatic "No, and" skews it toward escalation (read it as a threat, often raising Heat). *(Errata R-22 — skew is read off which emphatic answer fired, not off Triumph/Despair, for the same reason as §18.1 above.)*

## 20. NPC Quick-Generation

1. Roll 1d10 for **Archetype**: 1–2 Reich Official / 3–4 Informant / 5–6 Resistance Contact / 7–8 Civilian/Bystander / 9–10 Wildcard (interpret freely).
2. Roll 1d10 for **Disposition**: 1–3 Hostile / 4–6 Neutral–Self-interested / 7–9 Sympathetic / 10 Hidden agenda (Oracle: for or against the PC).
3. Build the stat block using the standard adversary framework:
   - Reich Official → Rival (2–3 threat)
   - Informant → Minion or Rival
   - Resistance Contact → Rival, often with one unique talent
   - Nemesis reserved for named, recurring antagonists only
4. Assign a Motivation (§13.4), reflavored: Desire — survival/ideology/power; Fear — exposure/reprisal; etc.
5. For a fuller personality (recurring/named NPCs), layer on the **NPC Behavior Generator (§39)** — Personality Type, Emotional State, Motive, Method, and Tilt.

# PART IV — RUNNING THE GAME

## 20A. Session Zero & Rule Zero

**Session Zero (before play begins):** agree as a group on expectations before the first real session. Discuss: acceptable language/content boundaries (especially relevant given the setting's real-world atrocity backdrop — agree explicitly on how far to depict Reich violence, and give players a way to flag topics as off-limits or "warn me first" privately); table logistics (breaks, food, devices); and any real-world-sensitive themes (genocide, persecution, collaboration) the group wants softened, spotlighted, or avoided entirely. Revisit this conversation if the campaign's tone shifts.

**Safety structure, explicitly:**
- **Lines:** topics that don't appear in the game at all, no exceptions. State these plainly in Session Zero and don't require justification.
- **Veils:** topics that can happen but aren't depicted in detail — "fade to black" and move on. Useful for content that's setting-appropriate but not something the table wants played out scene-by-scene.
- **Safety signal:** agree on a simple in-session signal (a word, a hand gesture, an index card on the table) any player can use to pause, skip, or soften a scene in the moment, without needing to explain why. Honor it immediately, no questions asked during play.
- **Debrief:** after any session touching heavy material, take a few minutes out-of-character to check in — how people are feeling, whether anything from the session needs a line/veil added retroactively for future sessions.

**Rule Zero:** the GM may override, skip, or reinterpret any rule when it serves the story or the table's fun better than a strict reading — including everything in this manual. Use sparingly and transparently (say "I'm using Rule Zero here" rather than silently reinterpreting) so player trust in the system isn't eroded.

## 20B. Constructing Encounters & Adventures

**Sizing an encounter:** the main lever is adversary mix (§12C). For a party of 4 PCs:
| Setup | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| 4 minions acting individually | Easy — good opener |
| 2 minion groups (3–4 each) | Easy–moderate |
| 1 rival + 1 minion group (4) | Moderate |
| 3 rivals | Moderately difficult |
| 1 nemesis + 2 minion groups | Difficult |
| 1 nemesis + 1 rival + 1 minion group (5) | Difficult |

Other difficulty levers: environment (cover, concealment, terrain — §5E), bystanders (crowds, allies, potential reinforcements), and exits/entrances (escape routes, how blockable they are).

**Sizing an adventure:** a one-session adventure works well with 2 major encounters plus 2–3 quick ones. A longer adventure runs 6–9 encounters across 3–4 major scenes. Mix combat, social, and exploration encounters — don't rely on combat alone. Resource depletion (wounds, strain, Heat, ammo) is the core pacing tool: encounters in rapid succession without recovery time build tension, but overuse causes frustration — balance accordingly.

**Letting the dice drive the story:** encourage players to propose their own Advantage/Triumph/Threat/Despair spends (§5C, §11) rather than dictating every outcome yourself — this gives players ownership and often produces better twists than pre-planned ones.

**Multi-location campaigns:** for a travel-structured campaign (moving between safehouses, cities, or borders), see the **Journey Framework (§34)** for Stops, Blockers, and Countdowns, plus the **Travel Encounter Table (§35)** for the road between them.

## 21. Session Setup (once, per campaign or one-shot)

1. Pick party framework: 1 PC (solo) or 2–5 PCs (group).
2. Assign career (§14) + 70 XP (§13).
3. Set Heat = 0 for the party.
4. Define the starting scene: location, immediate goal, one NPC present (Quick-Gen §20 if needed).

## 22. Group Play Loop (per scene)

1. **Frame the scene** — GM states location, stakes, active NPCs.
2. **Narrative or structured?** — switch to structured (rounds, initiative, §5) if there's conflict or time pressure; otherwise resolve with narrative skill checks.
3. **Resolve checks** — standard dice pool (§2). In a surveilled context, apply the Heat rule (§17.1) on Despair.
4. **Check Heat thresholds** — after any Heat change, immediately apply the relevant threshold effect (§17.3).
5. **Advance/end the scene** — GM cuts to the next scene or ends the session; award XP per standard practice plus a bonus for playing to Motivation.

## 23. Solo Play Loop (per scene)

1. **Frame the scene** yourself: where your PC is, what they want.
2. **Ask a question** whenever the outcome is uncertain and no PC roll directly applies → run the **Oracle** (§18).
3. On either emphatic Oracle answer — "Yes, and" or "No, and" (§18.1) → roll the **Random Event** table (§19) to inject content.
4. **PC actions** → resolve with standard skill checks exactly as in group play (§22 steps 2–4).
5. **Generate NPCs** as needed via Quick-Gen (§20).
6. **Heat** is tracked identically (§17); at Heat 4–5, use the Oracle to resolve raid/arrest timing and outcome instead of unilateral GM fiat.
7. Repeat until the scene resolves, then reframe the next scene (return to step 1).

**Optional depth (Part V, adopt individually):** layer in the **Fate Question Focus (§18A)** for a richer read on Oracle rolls, the **Meaning/Element Tables (§15A/§15B)** for instant prompts, the **NPC Behavior Generator (§39)** for recurring characters, the **Conversation Generator (§40)** for dialogue scenes, and the **Journey Framework (§34)** with **Travel Encounter Table (§35)** if running a travel-structured campaign. None of these are required for the core loop above.

## 24. Ending a Session/Adventure

- Resolve any outstanding Heat: a PC at level 5 either goes underground (relocates; Heat resets to 2) or is captured (Oracle/GM determines: escape, turn, or removal from play).
- Award XP: standard session award plus a bonus for playing to Motivation.

## 27. XP Award Guidance

- Standard award: **20 XP per 3–5 hour session**. Scale to taste: 25 XP for faster advancement, 15 XP for slower; shorter sessions −5, longer sessions +5.
- Add a Motivation bonus: a small additional award (commonly 5 XP) when a player meaningfully plays to their character's Desire, Fear, Strength, or Flaw during the session (per §12B).
- No separate combat/kill-based XP — award is flat per session, reflecting narrative engagement rather than body count.

## 28. Tone Guidance (mixing grim, pulp, and espionage)

Reich '62 deliberately mixes tones scene-to-scene. Use these levers to shift tone without changing the rules engine:

- **Grim/oppressive register:** lean on Heat consequences (§17) landing hard and narrating their human cost; keep Critical Injury and social-check Despair results (§9, §11) undersoftened; slow scenes down.
- **Pulpy adventure register:** favor Triumph/Advantage spends that let PCs pull off dramatic saves (§5C, §11); allow more generous Boost dice for style/flair; let chases (§12) and called shots (§10A) run cinematic rather than grim.
- **Espionage/le Carré register:** foreground Oracle-driven uncertainty (§18) and slow-burn Heat accumulation (§17.1) over direct combat; favor opposed social checks (§3A, §11) over structured combat encounters; keep information asymmetric — let players (and, in solo play, the Oracle) discover facts piecemeal rather than all at once.
- **Switching mid-session** is fine and expected — a checkpoint bluff (espionage) can escalate into a chase (pulp) that ends in a raid with real losses (grim). Signal the shift narratively (pacing, description) rather than mechanically; the underlying dice math doesn't change.

## 29. Dread/Fear Checks (optional — for grim/horror beats)

When the PCs confront something genuinely disturbing (a discovered atrocity, a close brush with the regime's worst machinery), call for a **Discipline check** ("a dread check"). Set difficulty by severity; once rolled for a given circumstance, don't re-roll it again in the same scene.

| Severity | Difficulty | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Startled | Easy(💠) | A sudden raid alarm, a body glimpsed briefly |
| Shaken | Average(💠💠) | Discovering evidence of a specific atrocity; a close personal betrayal |
| Deeply Disturbed | Hard(💠💠💠) | Witnessing mass violence directly; confronting one's own complicity |
| Traumatized | Daunting(💠💠💠💠) | Reserved for the campaign's darkest, rarest beats |

**On failure or 🔻🔻/⚡:** GM picks a fitting effect — Disoriented until end of encounter (simple failure); suffer strain equal to the check's Difficulty dice count (moderate); or, for severe failure, roll on the **Mental Trauma Table (§38)** for a lasting narrative scar rather than freeforming one. **On success or ☀️:** the character pushes through — no penalty, and an uncanceled ☀️ can instead grant 🎲Boost on the character's very next check as grim resolve takes over.

Use this sparingly — it's an optional lever for grim/horror beats (§28), not a constant tax on every dark scene.

## 31. CELL TRUST/TENSION SYSTEM

Fills a gap the Manual's Heat system doesn't cover: friction *within* the party, not pressure from the Reich.

- Each pair of PCs (or a PC and a key recurring NPC ally) tracks a **Tension rating, 0–2**, independently in each direction if the relationship is asymmetric (A's tension toward B need not equal B's toward A).
  - **0:** no tension.
  - **1:** suppressed feeling — irritation, unspoken loyalty, unresolved history.
  - **2:** uncontained — open conflict, obsessive loyalty, real fear of one another.
- **Effect:** in an opposed check between two characters with Tension toward each other (social or combat), the higher-Tension side adds 🎲Boost per point of Tension.
- **Raising Tension:** GM or player proposes +1 when a scene meaningfully strains or charges the relationship (a betrayal, a rescue, a hard call under pressure); requires the other player's/GM's agreement.
- **Reducing Tension:** both parties agree to reduce their mutual Tension by 1 (roleplayed as a scene of reconciliation, confrontation, or release) — doing so lets **each** participant recover 2 strain, mirroring §5G's strain-recovery scale.
- **Interaction with Heat:** Tension is orthogonal to Heat (§17) — a cell can have high external Heat and low internal Tension (a tight, trusting team under pressure) or the reverse (a fracturing cell that hasn't drawn Reich attention yet). Both can be dramatically useful at once.


## 33. PERSONAL THREAT COUNTDOWN

Gives each PC a personalized antagonist thread independent of party-wide Heat (§17).

- At character creation (alongside Motivation, §12B/§13), each player names one **Personal Threat**: a specific person, unit, or circumstance hunting or endangering their character specifically (an old SD file with their name on it, a jilted informant, a family member used as leverage).
- The Personal Threat has a **3-step Countdown**, advanced by GM discretion when the fiction calls for it (roughly once per session at most, tied to Despair results or major failures involving that PC):
  1. **Step 1 — Noticed:** the Threat becomes aware of/reactivated toward this PC. Narrative foreshadowing only.
  2. **Step 2 — Closing In:** the Threat takes concrete action — surveillance, an approach, a warning shot. Mechanically: add 🎲Setback to this PC's checks made to avoid/evade the Threat specifically.
  3. **Step 3 — Confrontation:** a direct scene between the PC and their Threat — this should be played out fully, not summarized, and typically resolves (temporarily or permanently) that character's Countdown arc.
- **De-escalation:** the Countdown isn't one-way. A PC may reduce their Personal Threat Countdown by 1 step through a significant in-fiction success against it — turning an informant, destroying incriminating evidence, striking a deal, or otherwise genuinely addressing the threat rather than just delaying it. Requires GM agreement that the action meaningfully counts, not merely avoids the issue for now.
- After Step 3 resolves, the player may retire the Threat or (with GM agreement) escalate it into a new 3-step arc — useful for turning a Rival into a recurring Nemesis-tier antagonist (§12C).

## 34. JOURNEY FRAMEWORK (Stops, Blockers, Countdowns)

An episodic campaign structure for movement through occupied territory — between safehouses, across borders, city to city.

**Time units for travel pacing:** this framework uses three loose scales — a **Round** (seconds, combat, per §5), a **Scene** (minutes to hours, one self-contained beat of play — a checkpoint, a conversation, a search), and a **Shift** (roughly 5–10 hours — a leg of travel, a stretch of rest, the gap between Stops). Shifts are the unit the Travel Encounter Table (§35) and downtime recovery (§5G) run on; they're narrative bookkeeping, not a strict clock — the GM calls when a Shift has passed.

**Setup (once per campaign or major arc):**
1. Choose a **Start** and **Destination** (e.g., a compromised cell's current city → a border crossing to a neutral country).
2. Choose journey length:

| Length | Stops |
|---|---|
| One-shot | 1 |
| Short | 2–4 |
| Medium | 4–7 |
| Long | 8–12 |

*When uncertain, default to the low end of the chosen range* (One-shot 1, Short 2, Medium 4, Long 8) — it's easier to extend a journey that's going well than trim one that's dragging.

3. Generate each **Stop** using this manual's existing Element Tables (§15B: Location, Faction, Complication) plus a **Blocker** (below).
4. Between Stops, roll on the Travel Encounter Table (§35).

**Blocker:** the obstacle preventing the party from simply passing through a Stop — a washed-out bridge requiring a specific document, a hostile faction controlling the only route, a Heat-triggered dragnet (Bestiary §6, "Manhunt / Dragnet" encounter block — not to be confused with this Manual's own §6, Derived Attributes). Generate via a 1d10 roll on the Element Tables' Faction/Complication lists (§15B) — flip a coin (or odd/even on any die) to pick which of the two lists to draw from — or invent one matching the Stop's location type.

**Stop Countdown:** each Stop has its own escalating pressure, similar to Heat thresholds but scene-local rather than persistent. Roll or choose a 1d10 **Countdown Event** whenever the party lingers at a Stop past its natural resolution point (GM discretion, roughly one per scene of delay):

| Roll | Event |
|---|---|
| 1 | Local tactics/patrol pattern changes |
| 2 | An ally or bystander is captured |
| 3 | Something the party needs is sabotaged or seized |
| 4 | Someone pleads for the party's help, complicating departure |
| 5 | The party is accused of something (true or false) |
| 6 | Victims of the Stop's central conflict appear, demanding attention |
| 7 | The party is directly threatened |
| 8 | The Blocker's controlling faction shows its full strength |
| 9 | The Blocker actively attacks or moves against the party |
| 10 | A deal is offered — favorable terms with a hidden cost |

## 38. MENTAL TRAUMA TABLE (d100)

A companion to the Critical Injury table (§9) and Dread Checks (§29), for lasting psychological consequences of the occupation rather than physical injury. Roll when a Dread Check fails severely (🔻🔻🔻/⚡) or when the GM calls for a lasting consequence to a traumatic scene.

| Roll | Trauma | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 01–15 | **Hypervigilance** | 🎲Setback on checks to relax/rest; recover strain at half rate (round down) until addressed |
| 16–30 | **Intrusive Memory** | Once per session, GM may impose 🎲Setback on a check when a trigger is narratively present |
| 31–45 | **Avoidance** | +1 difficulty on checks made to revisit a location/situation resembling the traumatic event |
| 46–60 | **Numbness** | 🎲Setback on all social checks involving genuine emotional expression, until addressed |
| 61–75 | **Compulsion** | Character develops a coping compulsion (substance, ritual, control-seeking) — GM and player agree on a concrete behavior; resisting it in a relevant scene requires a Discipline check |
| 76–85 | **Paranoia** | 🎲Setback removed from checks to notice threats (hypervigilant), but 🎲Setback added to Charm/Negotiation checks (visibly on edge) |
| 86–95 | **Dissociation** | Once per session, GM may declare the character loses a maneuver or acts confused for a moment during high-stress scenes |
| 96–100 | **Profound Break** | GM and player collaboratively define a significant, campaign-relevant change to the character's outlook or capabilities — treat with care and player buy-in |

**Addressing trauma:** requires sustained downtime (multiple sessions of narrative rest, trusted support, or in-fiction therapy/community care) and is resolved narratively between player and GM — there is no fixed dice-check "cure," consistent with the Manual's wellbeing-conscious treatment of Dread (§29).
